# Campus Matcha — campaign development

Created October 16, 2026. Five alternative fictional identities for ONE eventual creator; no identity selected or account launched.

## Objective
Develop an attractive 20-year-old mixed Asian and white college woman: petite, recreational gymnast, part-time boba barista. Attract an adult male audience while building a beauty and wellness audience that can support makeup, matcha, Pilates, activewear and lifestyle partnerships. The user clarified that “male energy” means audience appeal; no men's energy product is in scope.

Positioning: **The campus girl balancing class, the tea counter, and movement—with a good lip combo and a sense of humor.**

All family backgrounds, majors and personalities are fictional creative choices, not claims about ethnicity. Heritage should enrich her story rather than serve as a promise of attractiveness. Treat her as a clearly adult woman. Gymnastics is recreational; do not invent a real university affiliation, scholarship, team membership or competition results.

## Audience and content
Primary planning audience: adults 18–29 interested in campus lifestyle, beauty, drinks and movement; explicitly include women from the beginning. Male attention is an acquisition hypothesis, not the only success metric. Do not build months of unrelated thirst content and assume that audience will buy cosmetics later.

Starting editorial mix, to test rather than treat as a benchmark:
- 35% practical beauty: lip combinations, getting ready, makeup bag organization, color comparison.
- 25% boba/matcha: preparation, texture, drink choices, cafe stories.
- 25% gymnastics/Pilates lifestyle: bag packing, coordination humor, class preparation.
- 15% campus personality: study routines, friendly banter, small everyday mishaps.

Attraction cues: relaxed camera eye contact, expressive smiles, flattering ordinary clothing, confident posture, genuine-seeming comic timing. Retention cues: recurring stories, competent demonstrations, specific opinions, recognizable styling. Commercial cues: clear product shots, relevant uses, useful details and a proportionate call to action.

## Initial format tests
1. Beauty: “Three lip colors. One campus outfit.” Show the color comparison immediately.
2. Matcha: “The clumps are ruining your matcha.” Show whisking and texture, without health claims.
3. Movement: “Gymnast confidence. First Pilates class humility.” Playful fictional scene, not exercise instruction or a real attendance claim.
4. Boba: “Your order has main-character confidence.” Warm fictional barista comedy; no shaming customers.
5. Product demo: “Here is what actually fits in this tiny bag.” Use verified dimensions and real product footage.

Use native 9:16 footage with captions kept inside UI safe areas. Start with a visible action/product or clear premise, then develop one idea. The character sheets are casting assets; create a consistent single-scene phone-photo reference from the selected sheet before video production, as PIPELINE.md recommends.

## Partnership model
Offer clearly disclosed virtual-creator UGC-style creative: product demonstrations, scripted lifestyle scenes, hook variations, and edits for brand channels. Sell the creative service separately from organic audience reach. Never represent the fictional character as a real customer with real wear-test results or a genuine personal product experience.

Makeup and product textures/colors must use accurate assets, not AI-invented proof. A future wear test needs a real tester and documented conditions. Use AI and commercial-content disclosures where required; see research.md for official sources. Brand names, prices, results and logos are not invented in this package.

## Fair comparison plan
Produce three matched 15–25 second concepts per identity: beauty comparison, matcha preparation, and campus/movement comedy. Keep script, product, framing, duration, CTA and production quality comparable across the five; vary only identity and delivery first. This is 15 test creatives, not a posting commitment.

Evaluate 2-second and 6-second retention where available, average watch percentage, completion, saves/shares per 1,000 views, profile-to-follow conversion, product questions, and link clicks per view. Inspect audience gender/age only from aggregate platform analytics; do not infer demographics from comments or faces. For brand fit, track engagement and clicks from the intended beauty/wellness audience where reporting supports it.

Organic posting is not a randomized experiment. Rotate time slots, repeat concepts, and do not choose a winner from one viral post or tiny conversion counts. High male attention with few relevant saves or clicks means the hook is working but the commercial positioning needs adjustment. No numerical success thresholds are asserted without account baselines.
