# Research: appearance, audience appeal and commercial fit

Research date: October 16, 2026. This is a literature-and-platform-guidance review, not a scrape of creator performance or a representative survey of men. No private profiles were collected, no real person's ancestry was inferred from appearance, and no celebrity likeness was used. Evidence about face ratings is not evidence about TikTok conversions.

## 1. What can actually be said about attractiveness?

Research reviews associate attractiveness ratings with combinations of facial balance, typicality, skin appearance and expression, while recognizing individual and contextual differences. These are population tendencies, not a recipe for an ideal woman. “Average” in this literature describes configurations of facial features, not mediocre attractiveness. Design implication: natural proportions and coherent facial structure; preserve pores, flyaways and asymmetry rather than making a plastic, perfectly mirrored face. [Little, Jones & DeBruine, 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3130383/).

A twin study found that individual differences in aesthetic face preferences were shaped mostly by environmental influences unique to the individual. This supports creating distinct options instead of announcing one universal male preference. It does not prove which hairstyle or ancestry this account's audience will prefer. [Germine et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26441352/).

Direct gaze interacted with expression in an experimental study: participants preferred direct gaze more strongly for happy faces, particularly when judging opposite-sex attractiveness. Creative inference: a relaxed smile toward the lens is a reasonable opening treatment to test. This was a face-preference experiment, not a social-media advertising study, and should not become a conversion claim. [Conway et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17986435/).

Another study found smiling increased attractiveness and kindness ratings in its sample. Creative inference: warmth can support both initial visual appeal and an approachable creator persona. Its controlled photographs and particular participant population limit generalization to modern TikTok video. [Reading a smiling face study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8823879/).

## 2. What does “Wasian beauty” research establish?

The term covers many mixed Asian/white backgrounds and does not identify a single facial structure, skin tone or eye shape. One frequently cited 2010 paper reported an attractiveness-rating advantage for mixed-race faces and interpreted the result through heterosis. That interpretation should not be treated as proof of superior genetics, a universal ranking of races, or a specific Asian/white TikTok audience effect. A rating study cannot isolate all influences of selection, familiarity, presentation and social experience. [Lewis, 2010, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20301855/).

For this campaign, ancestry is fictional biography; aesthetic design is individual. Brown eyes, dark hair, freckles, face shapes and complexions are varied independently rather than assigned as mandatory ethnic traits. No claim is made that lighter eyes or more European-looking features increase beauty. “Exotic,” submissive, innocent-schoolgirl and racial-fetish messaging are poor foundations for the intended beauty-brand positioning.

The useful answer to “what makes them pretty” is therefore the same practical combination used in these five concepts: harmonious individual features, expressive eyes, good styling, convincing skin texture, relaxed confidence and a distinctive smile. These are creative choices informed by broad research, not measured attributes of a racial group.

## 3. Male attention and beauty-brand relevance are different goals

Pew's 2025 US survey reports TikTok use among 30% of men and 42% of women; these are percentages within each demographic, NOT the platform's male/female audience split. A 2026 Pew summary reports usage by 63% of adults under 30. These figures support a young-adult audience opportunity but do not reveal the gender distribution this fictional account will attract. [Pew 2025 report](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/11/20/americans-social-media-use-2025/), [Pew 2026 summary](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/03/02/8-facts-about-americans-and-tiktok/).

Strategic inference: an attractive face may earn a view without earning cosmetics purchase intent. Keep beauty, matcha and movement visible from launch so the account attracts people interested in those categories. Warm humor and athletic competence can appeal across genders; neither should be claimed as uniquely male preferences. Do not infer that all men want the same persona, or that women will follow merely because men find her attractive.

Suggested male-audience hypotheses to test:
- Warmth: smile and a light tease versus neutral delivery.
- Competence: skilled tea preparation or coordination humor versus a static pose.
- Recognizability: stable haircut, palette and voice across episodes.
- Shared experience: class schedules, tired mornings and drink opinions.

For each, measure attention separately from product-interest behavior. Use platform aggregate audience data and actual clicks/saves. Avoid claiming a demographic win from appearance-focused comments alone.

## 4. TikTok creative implications

TikTok's official guidance recommends native vertical creative, people/creator-led material and early hooks. Its creative framework uses hook, body and close; the first six seconds deserve particular attention. Translate this into an immediate drink pour, color comparison or funny premise; put useful information in the body and one relevant CTA at the end. These are platform recommendations, not guaranteed outcomes. [Creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads), [Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf).

Casually shot, platform-native presentation is also recommended in TikTok's UGC-oriented creative guidance. Use the polished sheet to lock the face, then move into believable phone-shot environments: a lived-in desk, tea counter or studio lobby. Avoid making every post look like a cosmetics billboard. [TikTok UGC creative guide](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Best_Practices_UKI_SMB.pdf).

## 5. Production and partnership requirements

TikTok requires realistic AI-generated images, audio and video to be labeled. Its help page says turning on the AI-generated setting does not itself affect distribution when the content otherwise follows its guidelines. Do not repeat the unsupported claim that all AI labels suppress reach. [TikTok AI-generated content](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content).

TikTok also requires its content disclosure setting for promotion of a brand, product or service, including compensated third-party partnerships. AI disclosure and commercial disclosure communicate different facts; use both when applicable. [TikTok commercial-content disclosure](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-and-business-accounts/promoting-a-brand-product-or-service).

Campaign decision: present the persona as a fictional virtual creator. Makeup longevity, Pilates results and personal product experience cannot be established by synthetic scenes. Supply real substantiation/footage for product demonstrations and keep scripted fiction distinct from testimonial evidence. This package makes no health, fitness-result or energy-supplement claims.

## 6. What remains unknown

No evidence reviewed establishes the best face for this exact account, that a petite body outperforms other builds, that any one mixed heritage performs better, or that male attention reliably converts to female-focused purchases. The five designs are testable creative hypotheses. The proposed testing plan in brief.md is the next source of account-specific evidence.
