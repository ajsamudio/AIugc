PLACEHOLDER NAME

Ray, 70s

Retired carpenter and high-school shop teacher. Gray beard, reading glasses on a cord, flannel over a white tee, worn work boots. Old pickup, porch, workshop, coffee in a chipped mug.

  • Sidekick: Biscuit, his old golden retriever. Free cuteness, and people will ask about him.
  • Voice: slow, warm, a little gravel. One voice, used for every video.
  • He never claims real-world credentials he doesn't have, such as military service, medical or financial expertise.
THE PROMISE

“Every quote is real.”

Wisdom TikTok is full of made-up and misattributed quotes over sad piano. Ray is the one who checks.

  • Every Real Quote video names who said it, where, and when, on screen.
  • “That's Not Who Said It” videos debunk famous misquotes (Einstein, Churchill, Gandhi). People argue in the comments, which drives reach.
  • Stories are real history: no invented “a man once told me” tales.
THE COMMUNITY

“Ask Ray”

People ask him life questions in the comments, and he answers with a video reply. This is the format that builds a loyal following.

  • Until real questions come in, use common ones (listed in the calendar).
  • Pin a comment on every video: “What should I ask Ray next?”

What “winning” looks like

  • Saves on Wish I Knew lists. This is the best signal for wisdom content; more than 3% of views is great.
  • Comment fights on That's Not Who Said It. Make it a twice-weekly series if they happen.
  • Watch time past 60 s on Stories. If people drop off at 20 s, move the punchline earlier and tease it in the first line.
  • Questions in the comments. Once you get more than you can answer, Ask Ray can become its own daily slot.

What to sell from Ray

Ray's products should feel like his own shelf: the books he quotes, things that last, gifts for dad. Book links work best on the same day he quotes that book. Book commissions are lower (often around 5–10%), but trust is high and the price is an easy yes. Check the actual rate on each listing.

Skip: knives and weapons (TikTok Shop restricts them), supplements or anything health-related, and financial products. Ray gives life advice, not money or medical advice.

Making it: the cheapest of the three accounts

  • Narration over B-roll instead of lip-sync. Ray is filmed doing quiet things (porch, workshop, walking, fishing) while his voiceover plays. No talking-head lip-sync, so he never looks uncanny and you don't burn credits on retries.
  • Build a B-roll library once: about 20 clips in the first week. Porch at sunrise with coffee, sanding wood, driving the pickup, walking a dirt road with Biscuit, fishing off a dock, by the fireplace at night, reading with glasses on, writing in a notebook, close-up of his hands. That's roughly 400 credits at ~20 per 10 s.
  • After that, each post is mostly new audio and text. Reuse clips in different orders. Add 3–5 new clips a week so it doesn't feel repetitive. Expect about 60–100 credits a week, far less than Tiff or Mr. Meaty.
  • Text style: big serif quote on screen, source in small type below (“Seneca, On the Shortness of Life, c. 49 AD”).
  • Sound: soft acoustic guitar or a crackling fireplace. Avoid the overused sad-piano wisdom sounds.

The quote-checking routine

  1. Find the quote in the original text (Project Gutenberg has free public-domain classics), or a speech transcript or letter.
  2. Check Quote Investigator for misattributions. The most famous quotes are the ones most likely to be fake.
  3. If the original can't be found, use “widely attributed to”, or move it to That's Not Who Said It.
  4. Translations differ (Seneca, Marcus Aurelius, Lao Tzu). Name the translation you used.

I picked quotes and stories in the calendar that are well documented, but check each one with this routine before posting. Some are marked with notes.

Rules

  • Label every post AI-generated, and put “AI character · every quote sourced” in the bio. Being honest about the character is what makes “every quote is real” believable.
  • Ray never pretends real events happened to him. He tells real people's stories, not fake memories of his own.
  • No stolen valor: on Veterans Day he honors real veterans' documented stories, and never claims to be one.
  • No medical, legal or investment advice. Money content stays at the level of habits (“pay yourself first”), never picking stocks.
  • Ask Ray replies to serious things (grief, crisis, abuse) get a kind line and a pointer to real help, not life coaching.
