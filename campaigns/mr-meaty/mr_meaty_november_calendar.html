Mr. Meaty November

Two felt puppets in burger-shop uniforms who keep turning up in the real world: silent, deadpan, and impossible to explain. Each one gets a single signature move, like Jean Phil's shadowboxing. A month-long workplace soap opera holds the account together, and green-screen templates let other people spread them.

Why Jean Phil blew up

Jean Philanthrope is an AI-made Frenchman: houndstooth suit, blonde poodle hair, handlebar mustache, shadowboxing silently in public. He went viral in September 2026. Strip away the hype and the recipe is simple:

  1. A silhouette you know in half a second. The hair, the mustache and the suit read even as a thumbnail.
  2. One repeatable action. He shadowboxes. Every clip is the same bit somewhere new.
  3. No dialogue. Silence works in every language, so it travels.
  4. Real-world, phone-camera footage. “Is he real?” arguments drove the comments.
  5. Remixable. Green-screen edits of his clips spread the meme further than his own posts.

Other recent AI hits fill in the rest. Italian brainrot showed that absurd names and catchphrases stick. Fruit Love Island showed that people will follow an ongoing AI storyline day after day and argue about it in the comments.

The two of them

Pick new names before you post. “Mr. Meaty” is a real 2006 Nickelodeon/CBC puppet show about two fast-food workers, and these sheets are close to its characters. Using that name and look could get videos taken down for IP and makes the account read as a knockoff. Below I use placeholders, Brisket and Slim at Burger Bunker; swap in whatever you like. Changing the visor and polo colors would help too.
SHORT · STOCKY · GINGER

Brisket

Round, red hair, bug eyes, pig nose. Very still, then does something sudden.

  • Signature move: The Shuffle. A stiff 3-second dance in a public place, then he walks off as if nothing happened.
  • Signature look: the long, unblinking stare straight into the camera.
  • Running gag: his name tag says “BRIAN” and he won't explain why.
TALL · THIN · BLONDE

Slim

Lanky, droopy hair over one eye, big lips. Calm and polite about everything.

  • Signature move: The Delivery. Walks somewhere absurd holding one burger on a tray, hands it over, leaves.
  • Running gag: No-Shave November. Starts with 3 hairs on Nov 11 and ends with a full handlebar on Nov 30 (a nod to Jean Phil's mustache, not a copy of him).

Ramp

Same as Tiff: 3 a day Nov 1–7 (Found Footage, The Bit, Lore), 4 from Nov 8 (+ Shift Cam), 5 from Nov 15 (+ Remix). Slots marked opt are skippable during the ramp.

What “winning” looks like here

  • Comments that argue “is this real?” mean Found Footage is working. Make more in that type of location.
  • Other accounts using your green-screen templates is the Jean Phil effect. Search the account name weekly and duet or repost the best ones.
  • Returning viewers on Lore (episodes 2–5 holding views close to episode 1) mean the storyline is working. If they drop off, make each episode work on its own.
  • If one Delivery or Shuffle location takes off, do 3 more like it in the next week.

Making it

  • They're silent, so no lip-sync. That's the cheapest, cleanest kind of Omni clip, and it matches what you already do (vibe-only, no mouthing). Every video uses one signature sound effect, like a fryer “ding”, plus trending audio.
  • Found Footage should look like a stranger filmed it: handheld, slightly zoomed, real places, a bit shaky. Prompt for “vertical phone video filmed by a bystander” and keep the puppets felt-textured and physical.
  • Shift Cam is the cheapest slot: a fixed high-angle CCTV shot with a timestamp overlay you add in CapCut. One kitchen setup reused all month.
  • Green-screen templates are made once and reused forever: Brisket's Shuffle, Slim walking with the tray, and Brisket's stare, each on solid green. Six or seven of these carry the Remix slot for the month.
  • Budget: about 2 new clips a day (Found Footage + The Bit), with Lore and Shift Cam reusing sets. That's roughly 300–400 credits a week, like Tiff.
  • Batch by location: generate 4–5 Deliveries or Shuffles in one sitting, then schedule them across the week.

Rules

  • Label every post as AI-generated. The “is this real?” debate is fun, but the label keeps the account safe, and people will argue anyway.
  • Don't use Jean Phil himself, his look, or his name. Borrow the recipe, not the character. He already has copycats, and being one of them is a dead end.
  • Stay away from meme coins. Jean Phil's virality got tangled up with a crypto coin. Never launch, hint at or promote one; it kills trust and invites scams.
  • Real-world settings should be generic places, not real identifiable people or real restaurant brands. Show no logos.
  • A big share of the audience is under 18. Keep everything PG, and don't aim shop links at kids.
